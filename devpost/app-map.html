How the app fits together

Paste a policy or tap a preset, and eight fictional Singapore residents react: who gains, who loses, by how much, and how it feels. Every number comes from tested code; the AI only reads the policy, writes the diaries and writes the neighbours' chatter. This page is a short guide to where things live, so you know where a future change belongs.

The main pieces

  • CodeThe town and the presets8 residents with budgets in 9 categories, and 4 hand-checked lever files. data/residents.ts, data/presets/*.json
  • CodeThe engineApplies levers in whole cents and gives each person a verdict from two lenses: % of income and % of spare money. lib/engine.ts, lib/format.ts
  • AIThe parserClaude Haiku 5.5 turns typed text into levers through structured output, checked by zod. lib/readPolicy.ts, app/api/parse/route.ts, prompts/parse-policy.md
  • AIThe diary writerClaude Sonnet 5.5 gets each resident's exact card numbers and streams one diary per line. lib/writeDiaries.ts, lib/diaryInput.ts, app/api/diaries/route.ts, prompts/write-diaries.md
  • CodeThe pageA controller hook owns the run; components draw the summary, map, chart, panel and cards. lib/useCrashTest.ts, app/page.tsx, components/
  • CodeThe charactersEach resident drawn in SVG from shared parts; the face follows the verdict, then the diary’s mood. components/ResidentAvatar.tsx, lib/expressions.ts
  • CodeThe town mapLights the places a policy touches, colours each pin by verdict, drops coins on one-off recipients and pops up reactions. It shows no numbers of its own. components/TownMap.tsx, lib/townMap.ts
  • CodeThe living townEach resident's day, walks along the streets, a 30-second day and night, and where people go when a policy lands (found by running the engine one change at a time). lib/townLife.ts, lib/useTownLife.ts
  • AIThe town chatterAfter the diaries, Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes 4–6 remarks between neighbours from their card strings; they're said one at a time in a feed and on the map. lib/writeChatter.ts, lib/chatterInput.ts, app/api/chatter/route.ts, prompts/town-chatter.md, components/TownChatter.tsx, lib/useConversation.ts
  • CodeAverages and what-ifsThe average resident against the real faces, sliders that re-run the town with no AI, and each person's tipping point. lib/average.ts, lib/whatIf.ts, components/AverageStrip.tsx, components/WhatIf.tsx
  • CodeYou, resident #9Your answers, checked by zod, become a resident with your own character. The server rebuilds you from the answers, and they go to Claude only for your diary. lib/yourself.ts, components/MoveIn.tsx
  • CodeA month in the lifeEach day's spare money as the change adds up evenly, and the day it runs out. lib/month.ts, components/MonthInLife.tsx
  • CodeThe Why? drawerA person's budget line by line and the two checks behind the verdict, from the engine's own category amounts (applyLevers) and lenses (verdictLenses). lib/why.ts, components/WhyDrawer.tsx
  • CodeThe phone map's focusWhere to centre the map for a policy or a conversation, and how far to scroll. lib/mapFocus.ts, in components/TownMap.tsx
  • CodeThe guided introA first-visit scroll story built from the fares preset and the engine, with no AI calls. Skippable at every step and replayable from the header. components/GuidedIntro.tsx

One action, end to end: tapping “Bus and train fares rise 20%”

  1. You tap the chip. components/PolicyInput.tsx: the chip’s onPreset(preset.id)
  2. A run starts with the hand-checked levers. No AI reads a preset. lib/useCrashTest.ts: runPreset → startRun
  3. The engine runs for all 8 people, instantly in the browser. Uncle Raju: −$12 is 12% of his $100 spare money, past the 10% line, so he “feels it”. lib/engine.ts: runTown → runResident → verdict
  4. You see the spread. “2 feel it, 4 barely affected, 2 unaffected”, the chart, the panel, and every card in its place, with Chloe and Raju now looking worried. lib/format.ts: summaryLine, cardText, chartRows, spareBar; faces from lib/expressions.ts: expressionFor; drawn by components/StatusLine.tsx, ChangeChart.tsx, ResidentCard.tsx
  5. The town reacts. The MRT station and bus stop light up with “Costs up ▲”, every pin takes its verdict colour, and the riders walk along the streets to their stop: Chloe to the MRT, Uncle Raju to the bus stop. lib/townMap.ts: litLandmarks, coinRecipients; lib/townLife.ts: gatherings, walk; drawn by components/TownMap.tsx
  6. Diaries are requested. The server re-runs the engine (it never trusts the browser’s numbers) and sends Claude Sonnet 5.5 each card’s exact strings. lib/useCrashTest.ts: requestDiaries → app/api/diaries/route.ts → lib/writeDiaries.ts: writeDiaries, with lib/diaryInput.ts: residentBlock
  7. Each diary drops into its card as its line arrives; a newer run drops late lines, and after 20 seconds anything missing shows “Diary didn’t come through”. components/DiaryNote.tsx
  8. The neighbours talk. Once the diaries are in, the server sends Claude Sonnet 5.5 every card’s strings and where each person is standing; remarks stream back one per line. If chatter fails, nothing else changes. lib/useCrashTest.ts: requestChatter → app/api/chatter/route.ts → lib/writeChatter.ts, with lib/chatterInput.ts: buildChatterInput
  9. One remark at a time, as a speech bubble over the speaker, a dashed line to the listener, and a line in the chatter feed. lib/useConversation.ts; drawn by components/TownMap.tsx and components/TownChatter.tsx

Black edge: plain code. Yellow edge: calls the Anthropic API. Green edge: what you see. A typed policy adds one step before step 2: runTyped posts to app/api/parse/route.ts, which calls readPolicy (Claude Haiku 5.5) and returns levers; from there the path is the same.

A three-stop reference route

A route to read in your editor; it wasn’t toured together. Follow the fares example through these stops:

  1. lib/useCrashTest.ts, search function startRun. Where a policy becomes a run: it cancels the previous diary request, runs the engine for all 8 people, and starts the new diary request. runTyped just above it is the typed-policy door into the same function.
  2. lib/engine.ts, search export function verdict. The kernel. It works in “×12 cents”, so a one-off counts as value ÷ 12 with no fractions. It compares by cross-multiplying, so landing exactly on −1%, −5%, −10% or −30% keeps the milder verdict. The worse of the two lenses wins, and the spare-money lens applies only to losses.
  3. tests/presets.test.ts, search Uncle Raju feels it. The PRD’s Expected Preset Results, written as tests: “−$12”, “0.9% of income”, “$12 = 12% of what’s left each month”. npm test proves the cards still say exactly that.

A practice to reuse

When an LLM eval fails, read the actual outputs before you touch the prompt.

The first live eval run failed 1 of 10 parser cases and 4 of 8 diary runs. The outputs told a different story from the scores:

  • The parser wrote tagsAny: ["has_children"] where the fixture expected mustHaveChildren: true. The engine selects the same parents either way.
  • The diaries quoted the policy’s own “$300” and “$3,000”, and one did correct arithmetic: Nurul’s spare money going from $500 to $900.

None of these was a wrong number on screen. So the fixes went into the evals and the input, not the prompts. The parser check now tests who is eligible rather than which field was used. The diary check allows amounts from the policy text. And the diary writer now gets the engine’s “spare money after”, so it never does sums. The re-run passed 10/10 and 8/8.

On your next project: test the behaviour that matters rather than its exact representation, and give the model computed facts instead of asking it to calculate. Then a failing eval points at a real problem. The same build used three layers of checks:

  • the shape is guaranteed (structured output plus zod);
  • the prompts are tested offline (tests/prompts.test.ts);
  • behaviour is measured live across repeated runs (evals/).

It happened again with the chatter. Its first eval dropped whole runs as “not JSON”, and the dropped lines showed why: the model copied the prompt’s {"speaker","listener","text"} shorthand literally. A placeholder example in the prompt fixed it (8/8). The same eval also flagged a “2 to 4 people” rule that the PRD never asked for; under fares, six neighbours naturally join in, so the rule went, not the conversation.

Evidence: devpost/checklist.md under “Revisions”, evals/parser.eval.ts, evals/diaries.eval.ts, evals/chatter.eval.ts, and lib/diaryInput.ts (residentBlock).
